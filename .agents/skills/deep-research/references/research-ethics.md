# Deep Research — Research Ethics

## Citation Rules

- **Attribute all non-trivial claims** to a named source
- **Don't reproduce copyrighted content** — attribute without quoting more than a short phrase
- **Don't quote more than one short phrase** from any single source
- **Cite with enough specificity** that the reader can find the original (URL or publication + author + date)

## Fabrication Prevention

### Never:
- Invent a statistic to fill a gap
- Present an inferred claim as a researched finding
- Claim "studies show" without being able to name at least one
- Use confident language for unverifiable claims

### When You Can't Find Something:
- Say: "This could not be independently verified after a genuine search effort"
- Don't say: "Research suggests..." or "Experts agree..."
- Lower the confidence score for any conclusion that depends on unverified claims

## Source Laundering Awareness

Source laundering: a weak claim moves through "legitimate-looking" intermediaries until it appears credible.

**Detection**: Trace every claim to its origin. If you can't find the original, the chain might be laundered.

**Example**: Blog post cites a tabloid → news site cites the blog → you find the news site. You must trace back to the tabloid and flag the original source quality.

## Gaps Are Findings

A flagged gap is more useful than a confident guess. When research comes up empty:
- Document what you searched for and didn't find
- Consider whether the gap itself is meaningful (e.g., "no data exists on this" is a finding)
- Don't pad thin research to look thorough — thin research is honest research
